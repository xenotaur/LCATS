# Heinlein Live Canary Report

This report covers `WI-SF-0113`: one flag-off baseline and three flag-on local trials of the optional `sf_heinlein` stage, run as `heinlein_canary_runbook.md` describes. It is an operational check of the stage and its failure handling. It is not a claim of theoretical accuracy, human agreement, or Phase 2 validation, and the pre-set expectations are operational checks, not gold labels.

## Recommendation: revise

Do not proceed to a larger sample yet. The stage ran without crashing and failed safely, but against this model its output cannot be interpreted, because the pipeline silently discarded the model's decisions in every run (finding 1 below), and because the shared evidence stage gave the Vonnegut story no usable evidence in three of four runs (finding 8 below). Fix that first, then rerun this canary.

**Correction, 2026-10-09.** The first version of this report said the shared evidence stage "succeeded in all four runs" and that the Vonnegut result was unexplained. Both were wrong in a way that matters. The stage ran without raising an error, but it produced zero usable evidence records for the Vonnegut story in trials 1 to 3 (finding 8), which explains the Vonnegut results. Findings 2, 4 and 5, the Vonnegut row of the expectations table, and "What to do next" are corrected below; the persisted result files were not changed.

## Findings

1. **The pipeline silently discards the model's decisions (main finding).** The tool schema names the keys `criterion_id`, `status`, and `supporting_evidence_ids` (`run_worldcon_spike.py:2473-2501`), and the normalizer reads those keys (`run_worldcon_spike.py:1966-1990`). In all six raw Heinlein responses, `gpt-oss:20b` used other key names: `criterion`, plus `decision` or `decision_state`, plus `evidence_ids`. The Knight stage in the same runs used the schema keys. The normalizer then finds no `criterion_id`, defaults every criterion to `not_assessable` with an empty rationale, and still reports the stage `complete`. No quarantine, failure, or coercion record is written. This contradicts the stage's own rule that decisions are never repaired quietly. The schema evidently was not enforced for this model through Ollama (not separately confirmed), so the prompt's "return exactly the schema keys" is the only control.
2. **The model's raw answers disagree with the stored verdicts.** For `anderson/bell` in trials 2 and 3 the raw response marks all five criteria `present` with one or two evidence IDs each, and the trial 1 fenced text does the same (all five `present`, one or two evidence IDs each). The stored result is `not_assessable` because of finding 1. The negative-control expectation is therefore satisfied by accident in trials 2 and 3, and trial 1 is not evaluable. If the keys had been read as written, the control would most likely have been `qualifies`, which would break its expectation; this is an inference from the raw decisions, not a computed result. For the Vonnegut story the model itself returned `not_assessable` for all five criteria in all three trials, with no evidence to cite, because the evidence stage gave it none (finding 8).
3. **Fenced JSON text instead of a tool call (trial 1, Bell).** The model answered with a Markdown-fenced JSON block and no tool result, giving a `JSONDecodeError` at character 0. This is the designed failure path and it worked: the stage was quarantined (`_quarantine/…/sf_heinlein.json`), the raw response was kept, the story rendered as `Unavailable`, the story still completed, and its Knight and Suvin results were unaffected. Trials 2 and 3 did not repeat it. Rendering was checked afterwards with `render_json(..., detail='detailed')` and `render_comparison_table` on the trial 1 sidecars: the Bell story shows `Heinlein Verdict: Unavailable` with the warnings "contains failure records" and "Heinlein analysis is not current and is not shown as a verdict", and the comparison table shows "Heinlein unavailable"; the Vonnegut story shows `Indeterminate (0 / 5 conditions)`.
4. **Partial successes elsewhere.** In the baseline, the Vonnegut Knight stage failed with a provider 500 (tool-call parse error, two attempts, both persisted) and its Suvin stage was quarantined for a `present` dimension without evidence. Both stages passed in all three flag-on trials. This is local-model variation in the Knight and Suvin stages, not a Heinlein result. Against the baseline, the flag-on trials' Knight and Suvin results are as follows (Knight interval is definite–possible of 7; no qualified Suvin Novum in any run): Vonnegut baseline Knight and Suvin failed, and trials 1 to 3 gave Knight 0–0 with Suvin complete; Bell baseline gave Knight 1–1 and trials 1, 2 and 3 gave 2–2, 1–1 and 1–1. So the Heinlein flag did not cause any Knight or Suvin stage to fail, and Bell's Knight interval varied once (trial 1), as ordinary model variation would allow. No dependency violation could arise: the raw Bell answers mark all five criteria `present`, and the raw Vonnegut answers are all `not_assessable`, so neither breaks the rules that essential and causal need different, or that causal needs human. Rationale-versus-evidence checking was not possible, because the stored result carries no rationale (finding 1). The Vonnegut Knight 0–0 intervals in trials 1 to 3 follow from the empty evidence set (finding 8), not from independent model judgments.
5. **Evidence stage: no errors, but not clean.** The shared evidence stage raised no error in any of the four runs, so the intermittent failure seen in `WI-SF-0015` did not recur as an error. That is one observation and does not show it is fixed. It did, however, return zero usable records for the Vonnegut story in trials 1 to 3 while reporting success (finding 8).
6. **Repeatability.** Trials 2 and 3 produced identical Heinlein output at temperature 0.0, with the same token counts. Trial 1 differed in the Bell response shape. Three trials of one model are not decision-grade.
7. **Stop conditions.** None of the explicit conditions fired: no stored `present` Heinlein decision lacked evidence (there were none), no dependency violation was accepted, every attempt has a persisted artifact, no paid call was made, and each trial was persisted before the next started. The runbook also stops when "provider-wide or infrastructure failures make a trial uninterpretable". Read strictly, that condition applies to every flag-on trial, because finding 1 makes the Heinlein output uninterpretable even though the runs succeeded technically. The cause was not understood during trial 1, which looked like a model that sometimes returned fenced text and sometimes declined to assess. It was recognised only while trial 3 was already running, after trial 2 had finished. Trials 2 and 3 therefore ran before the stop would have been applied. They are kept because they show that the problem is systematic: six of six raw responses used the wrong keys.
8. **The evidence stage gave Vonnegut no usable evidence in trials 1 to 3.** The sidecar `evidence_sets` show 7 usable records for the Vonnegut story in the baseline (1 candidate quarantined, "quote could not be located in prepared story") and 0 usable records in trials 1, 2 and 3, each with 6 candidates quarantined as `evidence_type is required`. In the trial 2 raw response the six items carry `quotation` where the evidence tool schema's key is `quote`, and have neither `evidence_type` (the schema's required key) nor `type` (`run_worldcon_spike.py:2371-2400`). The evidence builder coerces a model-supplied `type` into `evidence_type` and notes it (`src/lcats/analysis/science_fiction/evidence.py:90-95`), which is why the baseline's `type` keys worked, but these items had no such key to coerce. The evidence system prompt asks for an "exact quotation" (`run_worldcon_spike.py:2208`), so the model's key again echoes the prompt's wording; this is an inference, not tested. The quarantine was recorded in the sidecar, but the stage reported success, and Knight, Suvin and Heinlein then ran on an empty evidence set. That explains the Vonnegut results: Knight found nothing present, and Heinlein answered `not_assessable` for all five criteria because it had no evidence to cite. In trials 1 to 3 the Vonnegut evidence stage took the runner's text fallback every time (the forced tool call was not honored, logged as `no_tool_call_json_fallback`; `run_worldcon_spike.py:824-850`), while the baseline used a real tool call and got 7 records. That is a correlation across three trials, not a demonstrated cause. Wrong keys also appeared in real tool calls, for example Bell's Heinlein calls in trials 2 and 3, so the tool-call path is not protected either. `anderson/bell` had 3 to 4 usable records in every run. Whether the empty evidence set also explains the baseline's Knight and Suvin failures cannot be said, because the baseline had 7 records.

## What to do next

- Decide how the Heinlein stage should treat a response whose criterion keys do not match: fail loudly into quarantine, or accept the observed alias keys explicitly with a recorded coercion. Silent defaulting should not remain. Also decide whether to strip Markdown fences, and whether the prompt or a stricter local-backend schema forwarding should enforce the key names.
- After that change, rerun this canary: a baseline and three trials, with the pre-set expectations unchanged, and compare the Bell control and the Vonnegut positive again.
- Treat an evidence stage whose candidates are all quarantined as a stage failure, so later stages do not run on an empty evidence set, and align the evidence and Heinlein prompt wording with the schema key names. `WI-SF-0114` covers both, together with the Heinlein decisions above.
- Results apply to the current `heinlein-five-v1` rubric wording and prompt, and to this model and digest only.
