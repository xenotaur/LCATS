# Work Item: WI-SF-0123

## Summary

Write the design proposal for judging the five Heinlein conditions against the author's world at the time of writing, instead of against the date of analysis. The proposal specifies an optional analysis stage that estimates a composition-date interval, extracts the story's material factual premises with their change windows, and reports date-aware plausibility and relation-to-now flags, without changing the existing `heinlein-five-v1` record. This item produces a design for owner review and no code.

## Problem / Context

The `plausible` condition says "No established fact shall be violated" and the `different` condition says the conditions "must be different from here-and-now" (`src/lcats/analysis/science_fiction/rubric/definitions.py:123-176`). Neither text carries a date. The Heinlein prompt says to judge plausibility "against established facts available to a general reader" (`experimental/science_fiction_analysis_trial/run_worldcon_spike.py:2566`), which a model applies with today's knowledge. A story about a habitable Mars written before the 1965 Mariner 4 flyby would then fail `plausible`, a story about a technology that later came true would stop being "different", and a period detective story could count as "different" from today. The verdict is a conjunction (`src/lcats/analysis/science_fiction/models.py:362-377`), so one such failure changes the whole verdict.

The question came out of WI-SF-0119 (`experimental/science_fiction_analysis_trial/heinlein_canary_followup_bell_control.md`) and a manual premise trial on its six control stories (summarized in `heinlein_temporal_premise_trial.md`, created by this item). In that trial only one of six stories (A Case of Sunburn) has a plot-central physical premise whose truth depends on the date, two have dated future-history claims that have since expired, and two realist stories show that `different` has the same author-versus-reader time problem as `plausible`. The corpus records the story year as "Unknown" (`src/lcats/gatherers/parser.py:1457`), so dates must be estimated or sourced.

### Owner decisions already made (inputs to the design, not open questions)

1. The reference frame is "the here-and-now in which the story was written", for `different` and `plausible`, and by extension `essential` and `causal`. A today-reading is rejected.
2. The design is premise-first and date-second: list the material factual premises, record for each when its truth changed, and ask for a date only when a premise changes inside the story's plausible date window.
3. Knowledge changes over a window, from first credible evidence to consensus, not on a single date; a date inside the window is `ambiguous`.
4. The composition date is an interval. Sources, strongest first: owner-supplied; a bibliographic first-publication record; author lifespan and career bounds (the death date is a hard upper bound, with caveats for disputed attribution, collaboration, posthumous revision and translation); internal evidence the model can see. The sources are intersected, each with its basis and confidence recorded, and the owner can override.
5. A premise is judged over the interval: `present` if it holds throughout, `ambiguous` if it holds for part, `absent` if it fails throughout, `not_assessable` if no basis exists. Only central and substantial premises count; story-level status is `absent` if any counted premise is absent, `present` if all are present, otherwise `ambiguous`.
6. Each premise also gets a relation to the world today: stable, still open, realized, falsified, or calendar-expired.
7. Calendar-expired claims (a dated prediction whose date has passed) are reported through a separate flag and do not count toward the "plausible now" reading.
8. A story with no speculative premises has `plausible` present, and a separate flag records whether speculative premises exist.
9. The direction is a separate, opt-in stage with its own prompt version, leaving the five-criterion record untouched, phased toward a curated fact table as an audit layer. The result is a profile, not a score or a count, and profile categories are derived by Python.

## Risk Notes

- The trial is one rater and six control stories, mostly negatives, so the share of stories that need a date is unknown; the proposal must say so and recommend how to measure it.
- The design leans on a model's knowledge of when facts changed; an unreliable recall would make the date-aware readings look more certain than they are, which is why every claim carries a stated basis.
- Dates are hard to source for many stories; the proposal must make the cost of an unknown date visible (an `ambiguous` result with a stated condition) rather than hide it.
- A reference-frame decision can look settled and still conflict with the rubric's source text, which the repository holds only on the owner's report.
