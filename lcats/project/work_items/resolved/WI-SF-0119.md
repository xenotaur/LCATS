# Work Item: WI-SF-0119

## Summary

Explain, from the persisted rerun artifacts, why the Heinlein negative control (Bell) returned `qualifies` with all five criteria `present` in trials 2 and 3 but `does_not_qualify` in trial 1, and propose additional control stories with expectations recorded before any run.

## Problem / Context

The WI-SF-0116 canary rerun (`experimental/science_fiction_analysis_trial/heinlein_canary_report_v2.md`, PR 495) confirmed that the Heinlein stage now returns the schema keys, but the negative control met its expectation in 1 of 3 trials while the positive control (Vonnegut) qualified 3 of 3. The three Bell evidence sets held the same records, so the variation arises in the Heinlein stage or its run-to-run variation, and one negative control cannot say whether the detector discriminates at all. A single stochastic run is not decision-grade (WI-LLM-0059 makes the same point for baseline and modified prompt comparisons and requires at least three paired runs), so this item reads what the three trials already persisted instead of running more.

## Scope

- An offline comparison of the persisted Bell Heinlein raw responses, rationales, cited evidence IDs and evidence sets across flag-on trials 1 to 3 (excluding the flag-off baseline) under `results/worldcon_spike/heinlein_canary_v2/local/`.
- A written finding with the comparison method, and a design for two to four extra control stories; no new model runs and no manifest edits.

## Required Changes

1. Read the three Bell Heinlein raw responses and run-log events and compare, per criterion, the status, rationale, and cited evidence; check whether the evidence records cited as support actually bear on the criterion, and whether the evidence sets were identical in content and order.
2. Test candidate explanations against the artifacts only: rubric wording (for example the `plausible` slot's new-theory clause or the `human` and `different` conditions), evidence selection, prompt wording, or ordinary run-to-run variance. Label each explanation as supported, contradicted, or a hypothesis that needs a live run.
3. Propose two to four extra control stories (negative and positive) with, for each, the source of the expectation (owner-supplied or a cited text), the expected verdict, and why it is informative; record expectations before any run. Write the finding to `experimental/science_fiction_analysis_trial/heinlein_canary_followup_bell_control.md`.
4. State the live-run design a later item would need, without running it: every control story, new and existing, gets at least three flag-on trials under identical settings (same model digest, temperature, max tokens, manifest and flags); trial k of every story runs back to back, so the stories share the same server conditions per round; the comparison is the count of verdicts per story across trials, never a single run. This is defined here because WI-LLM-0059's paired-run rule covers baseline versus modified prompts and does not specify pairing for control stories.
