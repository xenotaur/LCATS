# Summary

# Result

Branch xenotaur/feat/wi-sf-0128, PR 547. New pure module composition_sources.py builds a CompositionInterval from owner-supplied dates, a bibliographic first publication (an upper bound only), author lifespan (birth lower, death a hard upper bound, career milestones deliberately not bounds) and internal evidence, intersected in the proposal's order, each applied source recorded with kind, value, basis and confidence. A conflicting weaker source is not applied and is named in a caveat; no source gives an unbounded interval, never a guess. Owner dates load from a strict reviewable JSON file (lcats-composition-dates-v1) and replace the computed result, recorded as an override with a caveat naming the interval replaced. 27 tests, none on the network.

The evaluation report (experimental/science_fiction_analysis_trial/composition_date_source_evaluation.md) could not evaluate ISFDB: its title and wiki policy pages returned HTTP 403 to the automated fetcher, which stopped without a workaround, and its robots.txt (re-read by me) reserves rights against AI use. Licence and terms are unconfirmed and the verdict is that it is not relied on. A second bounded pass evaluated Wikipedia, the SF Encyclopedia, Gutenberg's catalogue and Internet Archive for the ten sample stories: nine have a proposed first-publication date, Brown Wolf is unconfirmed. All page claims were read through a summarising fetch tool and are leads for hand-checking; the acceptance line about checking each date against a cited source is only partly met, and the report says so.

A cold diff-mode review found no bug in the construction logic; its findings (a vacuous test, an ISFDB overclaim, a collection count, wording) were fixed by the implementer before the first push.
